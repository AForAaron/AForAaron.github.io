What I want to find out

Ranking systems have been shown to prefer AI-generated text and images over human-made content that means the same thing. On shopping and video platforms, the item a user sees is now often part generated: a synthetic product photo above a description a seller wrote by hand. Nobody has checked what a multimodal recommender does with that.

  1. 1

    Is one generated modality enough?

    Earlier work looked at text alone or images alone. I want to know whether replacing only the photo already moves an item up the list.

  2. 2

    Where does the preference live?

    In the frozen visual encoder, the fusion layer, or the interaction history that feeds clicks back into training. The answer decides what can be fixed at all.

  3. 3

    Can it be removed after deployment?

    Without paired human and machine versions of each item, and without retraining the vision-language backbone. Influence-based unlearning is the first route I would test.

Builds on Dai et al., KDD 2024; Xu et al., SIGIR 2024; Zhou et al., SIGIR 2025.
